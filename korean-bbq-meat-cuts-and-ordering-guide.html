The Ultimate Korean BBQ Guide: Meat Cuts, Ssam Etiquette & How to Order Like a Local

Seoul barbecue is a meal you build as you go: sizzling meat, little side dishes and fresh leafy wraps. Whether you are eating in Myeongdong, Hongdae or anywhere else, this guide helps you choose cuts, order with confidence and enjoy the table.

Six Korean BBQ cuts to know

Look for 돼지 (dwaeji, pork) and 소 (so, beef) on the menu. Cuts, thickness and marinades vary by restaurant.

1. Samgyeopsal (삼겹살) — pork belly

The iconic thick-cut choice has visible layers of meat and rich fat. The fat renders as it grills, giving a savory, juicy bite with crisp edges. Grill thoroughly, then snip into bite-size pieces with scissors.

2. Moksal (목살) — pork neck

Leaner than belly but still juicy and meaty, moksal is a satisfying choice if you prefer less richness. Often served in thick slices, it needs time on the grill; let it cook through without drying it out.

3. Yangnyeom galbi (양념갈비) — marinated short ribs

Beef short ribs in a sweet-savory soy-based marinade deliver a tender, deeply flavored bite. The sugars can scorch quickly: turn the meat and ask for a fresh grill when the surface gets dark. Yangnyeom galbi can also be pork; check for 소갈비 or ask staff before ordering beef.

4. Chadolbaegi (차돌박이) — thinly sliced beef brisket

Paper-thin brisket curls as it hits the hot grill. Its delicate, tender slices cook quickly, with a rich beefy flavor; keep an eye on each batch and take it off promptly to avoid overcooking.

5. Hangjeongsal (항정살) — pork jowl / neck

This finely marbled pork cut is prized for its distinctive springy, lightly crunchy yet juicy texture. Brown it on the grill and cook it thoroughly; it offers a different bite from the softness of belly.

6. Dwaeji galbi (돼지갈비) — marinated pork ribs

These pork ribs are commonly marinated in a sweet-savory sauce for a comforting, glazed finish. The marinade can burn before the meat is ready, so turn frequently and ask staff for help if needed.

How to order and grill like a pro

  1. Check the menu first. Meat is commonly sold by serving (인분, inbun). Check serving weight, price and any minimum order; policies vary and some restaurants require more than one serving.
  2. Start with a contrast. For a group, try pork belly and leaner moksal, or add quick-cooking brisket. Ask whether mixed cuts are available and start with a manageable amount.
  3. Follow the staff's lead. Some restaurants grill and cut the meat for you; others expect you to handle the tongs and scissors. Ask when unsure. Thin beef cooks fast; sweet marinades can burn quickly.
  4. Keep raw and cooked food separate. Cook pork thoroughly, use clean utensils for cooked food and ask staff for a fresh grill plate if it becomes burnt.

If you have an allergy, tell staff and ask about marinades, sauces, banchan and cross-contact. Soy, wheat, sesame and seafood may appear where you do not expect them.

The art of ssam: build a one-bite wrap

  1. Place a lettuce leaf, a perilla leaf, or both in your palm.
  2. Add one bite-size piece of fully cooked meat.
  3. Add a small dab of ssamjang, a little grilled garlic and some scallion salad if available.
  4. Fold the leaf around the filling and enjoy in one bite. Keep it small enough to fit comfortably.

There is no need to wrap every bite: enjoy the meat on its own or with the dipping sauce offered. Avoid putting raw-meat utensils into cooked food or shared sides.

Banchan and refills

Banchan (반찬) are the small shared sides that may include kimchi, pickles and seasoned vegetables. They usually come with the meal, and refills are often free; ask staff or look for a self-service station. Policies and what is available vary, so check first. Free side refills do not mean free meat refills.

The finishing touch

Save room for doenjang-jjigae (된장찌개, savory soybean-paste stew), refreshing naengmyeon (냉면, cold noodles), or bokkeumbap (볶음밥, fried rice), sometimes made on the grill. Availability and whether these are included or ordered separately depend on the restaurant.

Korean phrases to show the staff

  • 여기요! (Yeogiyo!) — Excuse me!
  • 삼겹살 2인분 주세요. (Samgyeopsal i-inbun juseyo.) — Two servings of pork belly, please.
  • 불판 좀 바꿔 주세요. (Bulpan jom bakkwo juseyo.) — Please change the grill plate.
  • 상추 좀 더 주세요. (Sangchu jom deo juseyo.) — More lettuce, please.
  • 반찬 좀 더 주세요. (Banchan jom deo juseyo.) — More side dishes, please.
  • 이거 소고기예요, 돼지고기예요? (Igeo sogogiyeyo, dwaejigogiyeyo?) — Is this beef or pork?
  • 구워 주실 수 있어요? (Guwo jusil su isseoyo?) — Could you grill it for us?
  • 몇 인분부터 주문할 수 있어요? (Myeot inbunbuteo jumunhal su isseoyo?) — What is the minimum number of servings?

Frequently asked questions

What should I order for my first Korean BBQ?
Try samgyeopsal for rich pork belly and moksal for a leaner, juicy contrast. Ask about minimum servings and portion sizes before ordering.
Are Korean BBQ side dishes free to refill?
Banchan usually come with the meal and are often refilled for free. Policies and self-service options vary, so ask staff; meat is not generally included in refills.
Will the staff grill the meat?
It depends on the restaurant. Some staff grill and cut the meat; elsewhere diners cook it themselves. Ask for help if unsure.
Is yangnyeom galbi always beef?
No. Yangnyeom means seasoned or marinated, and galbi can be beef or pork. Check the menu or ask staff which meat is served.
How do I eat ssam?
Place cooked meat on a lettuce or perilla leaf, add a little ssamjang and optional garlic or scallion salad, fold it into a small parcel and enjoy in one bite.
