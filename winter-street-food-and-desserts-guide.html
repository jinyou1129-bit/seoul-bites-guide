Seoul Winter Street Food & Dessert Guide: Bungeoppang, Hotteok & Warm Comforts

Warm your hands around crisp fish-shaped pastries, sizzling hotteok and a cup of fish-cake broth. Stalls are seasonal and may move or sell out; confirm current menus and hours before a special trip.

Red bean vs custard bungeoppang: where to look

Pat (팥) is the classic sweet red-bean filling; shu cream (슈크림) is smooth custard. Neither name guarantees ingredients are dairy-free; ask the vendor about allergens. The fish shape does not imply fish in the pastry.

Start at Gwangjang Market South Gate 1 near Jongno 5-ga Station: Korea Times reported that Chonggakne Bungeoppang offered red bean and custard. For another area, browse the seasonal stalls around Myeongdong Station exits 6 and 8. Fillings and vendors change, so ask before queueing; these are leads, not a guarantee of stock.

Namdaemun seed hotteok

At Namdaemun Market, look for Namdaemun Specialty Hotteok: Korea Times reported a grain-seed option among its fillings. Seed hotteok (씨앗호떡) can contain nuts and seeds. Nearby Namdaemun Yachae Hotteok is instead known for savory glass-noodle-and-vegetable hotteok. Reach the market from Hoehyeon Station, line 4; check current hours and fillings at the stalls.

The syrup is extremely hot: let it cool before biting and take care with nut allergies.

Steaming odeng / eomuk carts

Follow the steam at market stalls or subway exits for fish cakes (어묵, eomuk; also 오뎅, odeng) simmering on skewers in broth. Ask before helping yourself to broth or sauces; practices and prices vary. Fish cakes contain fish and often wheat, and broths may contain seafood.
