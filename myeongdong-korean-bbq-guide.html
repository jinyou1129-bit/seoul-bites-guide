Myeongdong Korean BBQ Guide: How to Order Pork Belly & Hanwoo Beef

A tabletop grill, sizzling meat and a crowd of little side dishes can be intimidating on a first visit. Start with one pork cut, add beef if your budget allows, and let the staff show you how their grill works.

What to order: three cuts to know

  • Samgyeopsal (삼겹살): thick, fatty pork belly, usually unseasoned. Grill until cooked through and lightly browned; eat with salt, ssamjang or grilled kimchi.
  • Galbi (갈비): ribs, often beef or pork. They may be marinated in a sweet-savory sauce, so check the menu: sugar can scorch quickly.
  • Chadolbaegi (차돌박이): thinly sliced beef brisket that cooks quickly. Pick up a slice once cooked rather than leaving it to dry out.

Hanwoo (한우) is Korean native beef, often sold as premium cuts. Hanwoo is not the name of a single cut; check the grade, portion weight and price before ordering. Some beef on a menu may be imported.

How to order and budget

Menus typically price meat by portion, not by individual slice. Ask whether a two-portion minimum applies, whether rice or stew costs extra, and whether staff will cook at your table. For a simple meal, use approximately US $18–$38 per person as a rough planning range, not a quote; premium Hanwoo, drinks, larger portions and exchange rates can push the bill higher. Check the current Korean-won menu before ordering.

Grill and eat with confidence

  1. Let staff lead. Heat the grill or hot plate as directed. Many restaurants cook for you; if not, use the tongs and scissors provided, avoid crowding the grill and keep raw meat utensils away from cooked food.
  2. Cook pork thoroughly. Turn and cut thicker pieces as needed; ask staff if you are unsure. Do not eat raw or undercooked pork.
  3. Build a ssam. Place a bite-sized piece of meat on lettuce or perilla leaf, add a little ssamjang, garlic or scallion salad, fold, and eat in one bite. Adjust the combination to taste.
  4. Enjoy banchan. Small side dishes often come with the meal. Refills are commonly free, but policies vary; ask politely before assuming and order only what you can eat.

Where to start in Myeongdong

  • Wangbijib Myeongdong Main (왕비집 명동본점): 26 Myeongdong 8ga-gil. A well-known choice for beef and pork barbecue. There are several similarly named branches, so check the address when meeting friends.
  • Yuktongryeong Myeongdong Main (육통령 명동본점): 37-2 Myeongdong 8na-gil. Another central option to compare for grilled meat and current portion prices.
  • Wangbijib Myeongdong 2nd (왕비집 명동2호점): 13 Myeongdong 9-gil. An alternative branch if its menu or location suits you better.

Listings and menus change; check opening hours, wait times and the exact branch before going. References: Wangbijib and CatchTable restaurant listings.

Frequently asked questions

Can I eat alone?

Some restaurants have a minimum meat order or restrict solo grill seating. Ask before sitting, especially at busy times.

Is all galbi beef?

No. Galbi refers to ribs, and menus may offer beef or pork. Ask about the meat and marinade.

Can I avoid allergens?

Marinades and side dishes may contain soy, wheat, sesame or seafood. Communicate allergies clearly and verify ingredients with staff.

Do I need to tip?

Tipping is not generally expected in Korea; check your bill for any stated charges.
