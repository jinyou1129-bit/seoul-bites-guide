Best Korean Desserts & Street Sweets in Seoul: 6 Must-Try Treats

Kept in this browser only.

From snowy bowls of bingsu to a hot, syrup-filled pancake eaten on the street, Seoul’s sweets tell a delicious story of old traditions and new café creativity. Pick your team: red bean or custard?

1. Bingsu (빙수): shaved ice dessert

A mountain of delicate shaved ice is Seoul’s answer to a hot afternoon. Injeolmi bingsu combines nutty roasted soybean powder with chewy rice-cake pieces, often over milk ice. Fresh fruit bingsu adds juicy seasonal fruit for a brighter, refreshing bite. Classic patbingsu features sweet red beans. Bowls are often generous enough to share; toppings and dairy vary by café.

2. Hotteok (호떡): sweet stuffed pancake

Watch dough pressed golden on a griddle: the crisp exterior gives way to a chewy center and molten brown-sugar cinnamon syrup. Busan-style ssiat hotteok adds a crunchy mix of seeds, sometimes with nuts. This is a beloved cold-weather market snack, though availability varies.

First-bite warning: the syrup can be extremely hot. Let it cool and ask about nuts and seeds.

3. Bungeoppang (붕어빵): fish-shaped pastry

Made in a fish-shaped mold, this winter street classic has a lightly crisp shell and a soft center. Pat (팥) is earthy, sweet red bean paste; shu cream (슈크림) is smooth, sweet custard. Buy one of each to settle the debate. The shape is fish; the usual sweet filling is not. Check ingredients with the vendor.

4. Kkwabaegi (꽈배기): twisted doughnuts

Look for these twisted, fried doughnuts at traditional markets. A fresh one is fluffy and slightly chewy inside, with a golden exterior and a coating of sugar that crunches between your fingers. They’re a familiar market snack, best enjoyed soon after frying.

5. Yakgwa (약과): honey pastry

Yakgwa is a traditional Korean confection made with fried wheat dough and a honey or syrup glaze. Gently spiced and fragrant, it can be dense and chewy with a lightly crisp edge. Once a treat for holidays and celebrations, it now turns up in traditional tea houses and contemporary desserts alike. Pair it with a cup of tea.

6. Croffle (크로플): croissant meets waffle

A croissant dough waffle delivers a crisp, caramelized surface and buttery, flaky layers. Seoul cafés often dress this modern favorite with ice cream, fruit or syrup. It’s a playful contrast to the traditional sweets: order one to share after a café-hopping walk.

Where to look in Seoul

  • Insadong: stroll Insadong-gil for traditional tea houses and hangwa shops; look for yakgwa and seasonal hotteok. Korea Tourism Organization lists Hangwamiuisik at Annyeong Insadong for traditional sweets. Explore Insadong tea and snacks →
  • Myeongdong: browse the shopping-street stalls near Myeongdong Station exits 6 and 8 for hotteok and seasonal bungeoppang. Check what is available on the day. Explore Myeongdong street food →
  • Seongsu: explore the neighborhood’s contemporary cafés for croffles and seasonal bingsu; check current menus before making a special trip. Explore Seongsu cafés →
  • Traditional markets: look for fresh kkwabaegi and hotteok; Gwangjang and Mangwon are useful areas to browse. Stall schedules and offerings vary.

These are starting points, not promises of specific stock. Stalls can move, sell out or close seasonally; bring some cash in case cards aren’t accepted.

Frequently asked questions

What Korean desserts should I try in Seoul?

Try bingsu, hotteok, bungeoppang, kkwabaegi, yakgwa and croffles for a mix of traditional and modern sweets.

Where can I find Korean street sweets in Seoul?

Browse Myeongdong street stalls, traditional markets and Insadong. Menus, opening times and seasonal stalls change.

Are bungeoppang fish-shaped pastries made with fish?

The name and shape refer to fish; the usual sweet versions have red bean or custard filling. Ask the vendor about ingredients and cross-contact.

When is the best time to try bungeoppang and hotteok?

These warming street snacks are especially associated with winter, although availability varies by stall and season.

Are Korean desserts safe for people with food allergies?

Ingredients vary. Wheat, milk, eggs, soy, nuts and seeds may be present, and cross-contact is possible. Check directly with the vendor.
