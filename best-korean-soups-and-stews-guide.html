Best Korean Soups & Stews for Beginners: 6 Comforting Bowls in Seoul

Kept in this browser only.

From fiery kimchi stew to gently simmered beef broth, a Korean soup or stew makes a comforting meal after a day exploring Seoul. Here are six bowls to help you order with confidence. Recipes and heat levels vary by restaurant.

Six iconic Korean soups and stews to try

1. Kimchi Jjigae (Aged Kimchi & Pork Stew) 김치찌개

Well-fermented kimchi gives this bubbling stew a tangy, deep flavor; pork adds richness, often alongside tofu and onion. Its familiar sour-spicy warmth is everyday Korean comfort food. Spice: usually medium to hot, depending on the kitchen.

2. Doenjang Jjigae (Fermented Soybean Paste Stew) 된장찌개

Fermented soybean paste creates an earthy, savory broth, commonly simmered with tofu, zucchini, onion and other vegetables. It is a classic partner to rice and a home-style meal. Spice: often mild to medium; some versions include chili. Broth may contain anchovy, seafood or meat.

3. Sundubu Jjigae (Spicy Soft Tofu Stew) 순두부찌개

Silky, unpressed soft tofu rests in a rich, bubbling chili broth. Seafood such as clams is common, though pork and other versions are also served; an egg may arrive with the bowl. Spice: generally medium to hot, but varies. Ask whether heat can be adjusted and confirm seafood or egg if needed.

4. Seolleongtang (Ox Bone Soup) 설렁탕

Beef bones simmer for hours to make a milky-white, rich yet gentle broth, typically served with slices of beef and rice. Its mellow flavor invites you to season it yourself, making it a reassuring first bowl. Spice: usually not spicy unless you add chili or spicy condiments.

5. Samgyetang (Ginseng Chicken Soup) 삼계탕

A whole young chicken is simmered with ginseng and often garlic and jujube, with sticky rice tucked inside. The savory broth and tender meat make a nourishing, traditional comfort meal, enjoyed even in summer. Spice: usually mild and not chili-hot.

6. Galbitang (Clear Beef Short Rib Soup) 갈비탕

Beef short ribs are simmered until tender in a clear, golden, deeply savory broth; you may find radish, scallions or glass noodles depending on the restaurant. Enjoy the tender meat and the soothing broth with rice. Spice: usually mild; any spicy sauce or kimchi is often served separately.

How to eat Korean soups like a local

  1. Taste before seasoning. For mild soups such as seolleongtang, add a little salt and scallion if provided, then taste again. Not every soup needs extra seasoning.
  2. Pair with rice and banchan. Rice usually comes in its own bowl. Alternate spoonfuls of rice and soup, or add rice to your individual soup bowl if you like. Use your spoon for broth and rice, and chopsticks for solid side dishes; avoid placing used utensils directly in shared dishes.
  3. Mind the heat. A stone or earthenware bowl can remain extremely hot and may still bubble when served. Do not touch it; let the soup cool and take small spoonfuls.
  4. Check what is in the broth. A tofu or vegetable dish is not automatically vegetarian, and kimchi may contain fish sauce. For allergies, ask about ingredients and cross-contact directly.

Korean phrase cards to show restaurant staff

Point to a card when ordering. Restaurants may not be able to change a prepared broth.

Frequently asked questions

Which Korean soup is best for beginners who do not like spice?

Seolleongtang and galbitang are usually mild. Ask staff about seasonings and ingredients because recipes vary.

Are Korean soups and stews vegetarian?

Not necessarily. Even vegetable or tofu stews can contain meat, seafood, anchovy broth or fish sauce. Confirm ingredients and cross-contact with staff.

Do I put rice into Korean soup?

Rice is often served separately. Eat spoonfuls of rice and soup alternately, or add rice to your own bowl if you prefer; customs vary by dish and person.

Can restaurants make a stew less spicy?

Some can adjust the heat, but others prepare the broth in advance. Ask 덜 맵게 해 주세요 and check before ordering.
