Seoul Traditional Markets Food Guide: Gwangjang vs Namdaemun vs Tongin vs Mangwon

Kept in this browser only.

Four markets, four very different food adventures. Choose sizzling pancakes, noodle alleys, a brass-coin lunchbox or a takeaway feast — and arrive hungry.

Practical planning guide · Updated November 26, 2026. Hours, prices and stall availability can change; check locally before traveling.

Pick your market in 10 seconds

  • Gwangjang: iconic first-time street-food crawl.
  • Namdaemun: hearty noodle alleys and shopping.
  • Tongin: hands-on lunchbox near Gyeongbokgung.
  • Mangwon: crunchy takeaway and neighborhood cafe culture.

1. Gwangjang Market (광장시장): the classic feast

Go for: Bindaetteok (빈대떡), crispy mung-bean pancakes; yukhoe (육회), seasoned raw beef tartare; and mayak gimbap (마약김밥), small rice rolls often served with mustard dip. “Mayak” is a nickname, not an ingredient.

Start with a freshly griddled pancake to share, add gimbap and, if you eat raw beef, order yukhoe from a busy vendor. Pancakes may contain pork; ingredients and sauces differ. Raw meat carries food-safety risks, particularly for pregnant people, older adults and people with weakened immune systems.

Getting there: Subway Line 1, Jongno 5-ga Station, Exit 8. The official Seoul listing describes the food alley as open daily 09:00–23:00; individual vendors may differ. Arrive before lunch if you prefer more room.

2. Namdaemun Market (남대문시장): noodles and old-school shopping

Go for: kalguksu (칼국수), hand-cut noodle soup in Kalguksu Alley; galchi-jorim (갈치조림), spicy braised hairtail with radish; and hotteok (호떡), including savory vegetable-filled versions. Fillings vary by vendor: some hotteok are sweet with seeds, rather than vegetable-filled.

Sit down for noodles, then explore the hairtail alley. A braised fish order may require two portions, so ask before sitting. Kalguksu broth and side dishes can contain fish or other animal ingredients. Pair the meal with a walk toward Sungnyemun Gate.

Getting there: Line 4, Hoehyeon Station. Exit 5 is convenient for Kalguksu Alley; Exit 7 leads to the market. Individual schedules vary and many shops close Sunday; go late morning on a weekday for the best chance of finding a seat.

3. Tongin Market (통인시장): build a brass-coin lunchbox

Go for: yeopjeon dosirak (엽전 도시락), a DIY lunchbox, and gireum tteokbokki (기름떡볶이), rice cakes stir-fried in oil and seasoning rather than a pool of sauce. Ask whether yours is spicy.

  1. Find the Dosirak Café / customer service center inside the market and exchange money for yeopjeon tokens and a tray.
  2. Pick small portions from participating stalls displaying the lunchbox program sign; not every merchant accepts tokens.
  3. Return to the café to eat. Ask staff about current coin price, minimum purchase, remaining coins and seating rules.

Getting there: Line 3, Gyeongbokgung Station, Exit 2, then walk to the market. Plan around lunch: the café has its own operating days and coin-sale hours, separate from the market. Official Seoul pages currently give conflicting closure days and hours, so confirm the café schedule before setting out; popular items may sell out.

4. Mangwon Market (망원시장): crunchy bites and local cool

Go for: giant fried stuffed peppers (고추튀김), crisp dakgangjeong (닭강정, sweet or spicy chicken bites), croquettes and other snacks. Pepper heat and fillings vary; ask before ordering.

Take a few things to go, browse neighborhood shops and explore nearby Mangnidan-gil cafés. Mangwon Hangang Park is an option for a picnic; pack rubbish out and check local rules. Popular stalls can have queues or sell out.

Getting there: Line 6, Mangwon Station, Exit 2. The Seoul tourism listing gives market hours of 10:00–21:00 daily, varying by store. Aim for a weekday late morning or early afternoon.

Before you order: practical market etiquette

  • Check the displayed price, portion size and payment method before ordering; some sit-down dishes have a minimum order.
  • Point to a menu item or show the Korean phrases below. Ask whether food is for takeaway or eating at the stall.
  • Keep narrow aisles clear and dispose of packaging responsibly. Ask before taking close-up photos of people or stalls.
  • For allergies or dietary restrictions, confirm ingredients, broth, sauces and shared cooking equipment directly. A phrase card cannot guarantee safety.

Show a vendor: essential Korean phrases

Point to the food, then show the relevant line.

이거 얼마예요?How much is this?
이거 하나 주세요.One of this, please.
카드 결제 돼요?Can I pay by card?
현금만 되나요?Is it cash only?
포장해 주세요.To go, please.
맵지 않게 해 주세요.Please make it mild / not spicy.
돼지고기가 들어가나요?Does it contain pork?
엽전으로 살 수 있나요?Can I buy this with yeopjeon?

For a specific allergy, identify the ingredient and confirm cross-contact; do not rely on a generic phrase alone. Open our dietary card builder.

Frequently asked questions

Which Seoul market is best for first-time visitors?

Gwangjang is a classic first stop for bindaetteok, mayak gimbap and yukhoe. Choose Namdaemun for noodles and shopping, Tongin for a DIY lunchbox, or Mangwon for takeaway snacks and neighborhood cafes.

How does the Tongin Market lunchbox work?

At the Dosirak Café, exchange money for yeopjeon tokens and collect a tray. Choose portions at participating vendors, then return to the café to eat. Check current opening days and coin sales before visiting.

Can I use a credit card at Seoul traditional markets?

Payment varies by vendor. Ask before ordering and carry some Korean won in cash as a backup. Tongin lunchbox tokens work only at participating stalls.

When should I visit Seoul traditional markets?

Late morning on a weekday is a useful starting point for avoiding the busiest lunch and weekend periods. Individual stalls have different hours, and the Tongin lunchbox has a separate schedule.
