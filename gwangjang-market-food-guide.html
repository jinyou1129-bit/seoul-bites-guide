Gwangjang Market Food Guide: What to Eat and How to Order

h1>

Updated November 2026 • 6 min read

p>

Gwangjang Market (광장시장) is Korea's oldest continuous daily market, dating back to 1905. Under its covered canopy lies Seoul's most famous street food runway. Here is what to eat and how to order with confidence.

p>

Top 5 Foods You Must Try

h2>
  • Bindaetteok (빈대떡): Crispy, savory mung bean pancakes ground fresh on stone mills and shallow-fried in bubbling oil. Dip in onion-infused soy sauce.
  • Mayak Gimbap (마약김밥): "Addictive" bite-sized seaweed rice rolls filled with pickled radish and carrots, dipped into a sharp hot mustard sauce.
  • Tteokbokki (떡볶이): Thick, finger-length rice cakes simmered in a rich, sweet, and fiery gochujang reduction.
  • Yukhoe (육회): Fresh raw beef tartare seasoned with sesame oil and topped with a golden egg yolk and crisp Asian pear. Tip: Order only at dedicated, bustling sit-down butcher restaurants on Yukhoe Alley.
  • Kalguksu (칼국수): Knife-cut noodles served in clear, comforting anchovy broth with freshly made kimchi.
ul>

How to Order Like a Local

h2>
  • Shared Counter Seating: Look for an open wooden bench. If there's an open spot, point and sit down. Heated benches are common in winter!
  • Point and Gesture:strong> Stalls have photo menus and food displays. Pointing and holding up fingers for quantity works seamlessly.
  • Ask Price First: Most dishes cost between 4,000 KRW and 12,000 KRW. Confirm the price before you order.
  • Payment: Credit cards are accepted at larger stalls, but carry small cash (1,000 and 5,000 won bills) for small vendors.
  • Share Plates:strong> Don't fill up at one stall. Order one dish per vendor and graze across 3 or 4 stops.
ul>

Beginner Street Smarts

  • Timing: Visit off-peak (between 2:30 PM and 5:00 PM) to avoid shoulder-to-shoulder crowds.
  • Walking Gear:strong> The aisles are narrow and wet from market washing. Wear flat, non-slip walking shoes.
  • Hygiene: Carry hand sanitizer and pocket tissues; market napkins are often basic.
  • Allergies: Save a Korean note on your phone (e.g., "저는 땅콩/갑각류 알레르기가 있습니다").

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Gwangjang Market good for complete beginners?
Yes! While chaotic and vibrant, stall aunties (Imo-nim) are used to foreign visitors and very friendly.
Are there vegetarian options?
Bindaetteok and Mayak Gimbap are largely vegetarian, though some broths or kimchi contain anchovy or shrimp paste. Always double-check.
How much time should I allocate?
Plan for about 1.5 to 2 hours to walk, eat at 2-3 stalls, and soak in the atmosphere. Operating hours and prices may change over time.
